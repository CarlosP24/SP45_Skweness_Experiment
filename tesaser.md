# Teaser summary for PRB editor's suggestions
Full-shell nanowires, semiconductor cores wrapped in a superconducting shell, behave as synthetic vortices. They host Caroli–de Gennes–Matricon (CdGM) subgap states whose spectrum skews with axial magnetic field, in a way set by microscopic details such as the radial localization of the charge density, the chemical potential, subband occupation, and coupling to the shell. Here, the authors detect this skewness fingerprint in the critical current and zero-bias resistance of full-shell nanowire Josephson junctions, showing that transport can probe these otherwise hidden properties.
